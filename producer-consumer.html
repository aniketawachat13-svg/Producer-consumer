Event Log

1. What is a Producer?

A producer creates work and hands it off without doing the work itself. Each student is a producer: "Generate Print Job" drops a job into the shared queue.

2. What is a Consumer?

A consumer takes work out of the shared queue and processes it. The printer pulls the oldest job and prints it, one at a time.

3. What is the Buffer?

The shared print queue between students and printer. It holds up to 5 jobs and absorbs the speed mismatch between producers and consumer.

4. Why does the producer wait when the buffer is full?

A sixth job would overwrite or lose data. At 5 / 5, producers must wait until the printer frees a slot.

5. Why does the consumer wait when the buffer is empty?

The printer can't print a job that doesn't exist. At 0 / 5 it idles until a student submits something.

6. What is synchronization?

Rules that stop producers and consumer from interfering when sharing the buffer — no two writing the same slot, and no reading an unfilled slot.

7. What are empty, full, mutex?

empty = free slots (starts at 5). full = filled slots (starts at 0). mutex = lock ensuring only one side changes the queue at a time. They're shown live in the header, but this simulation doesn't use real OS semaphores.
